## 1. Guiding question and research questions

**Guiding question.** How do the controls of a regeneration workflow with separated roles operate when applied to the output of a research objective (OR3) of an industrial R&D project; which failures emerge in their application; which lessons follow.

- **RQ1 — Incidents.** For 3–5 incidents chosen with the criteria of §6: the rule in force, the observed sequence, before/after evidence, the control that intervened (version and date), the controls available at the time that stayed blind, the correction, the confirmation of the outcome, alternative explanations, transferability.
- **RQ2 — Corpus.** Classification of the whole frozen corpus (§3–§5, §8): type, phase of introduction, artefact, stage of detection, first observed detector, controls available at the time, potential impact. Includes a description of **how the problems of the controls themselves** (type 8) were discovered, with no statement to be tested.
- **RQ3 — Resources and rework.** Measured resources (machine hours from session logs), rework (repeated runs, tags), duration (dates). Timestamps describe elapsed time and recorded activity, not human working hours.
- **RQ4 — Assistants (secondary).** For each incident and for the corpus, which role was held by an assistant at introduction and at detection. Descriptive fact; no causal attribution.

## 4. Unit of analysis

- **Incident**: narrative unit (RQ1): a sequence rule → state → detection → correction → confirmation, reconstructed in a card.
- **Problem**: counting unit (RQ2): a distinct defect, deviation or violation. A register item may contain several problems; a problem may span several items, tags or records.
- **Status** of every candidate element, decided in phase 3 of §8 and documented: **defect** (an outcome different from what protocol, code or text prescribed); **deviation** (a declared post-signature choice with no outcome different from what was prescribed); **violation** (a process rule not followed); **excluded from the count** (improvement, change of specification, editorial note), with a written reason. «No effect on the numbers» is not enough to classify a deviation: what counts is whether the outcome differs from what was prescribed.
- Separation and deduplication rules: the same error propagated through several tags or records is **one** problem; a documentary error (wrong date, cause or attribution) is a problem distinct from the one it documents, linked to the same incident; a problem cited in both registers counts **once**, marked «shared», with both references; every decision to split or merge is written in the phase-3 minutes.
- Every problem has an ID (`P1-nn`, `P4-nn`, `PC-nn` for shared ones) and points to the items, the commits and the incident, if any.

### 5.2 Rules on sources

A statement is **documented** only if an artefact supports *that* statement; an artefact that concerns the same episode is not enough (a json recording machine and discrepancy does not identify the cause of the discrepancy). A testimony never yields a documented statement nor a register item: it yields a hypothesis and the search for the artefact. What remains without an artefact stays «hypothesis» or «attributed recollection», in the paper too.

### 5.3 Taxonomy of types (pre-specified before coding, informed by prior knowledge of the case)

Anchored to Kapoor & Narayanan (2023), REFORMS (2024), Arp et al. (2022); adapted to the case.

1. **Leakage**: test information in training, normalisation, weights or selection.
2. **Indexing and alignment**: windows, calendar jumps, joins, populations.
3. **Silent loss**: filters, masks, fallbacks that discard or substitute without flagging.
4. **Metric**: 4a declared metric ≠ implemented; 4b inadequate metric implemented faithfully; 4c scoring not comparable across configurations.
5. **Budget, selection and convergence**: epochs, checkpoints, thresholds chosen in sample.
6. **Unsupported statement in the text**: a sentence of the manuscript with no record supporting it, or exceeding the record.
7. **Provenance and traceability**: generator not found, number not traced, hash not linked; **7d documentary**: wrong date, cause or attribution in a register, protocol or note.
8. **Defective control**: 8a false positive; 8b false negative or blindness to a class of changes.
9. **Procedural violation**: role beyond its permissions, order of phases, signature.
10. **Environment and dependencies**: versions, third-party licences, non-determinism across machines.
11. **Other**, with a reason.

**Precedence rules for the primary type** (the first that applies): (i) if the problem is an error in a date, cause or attribution in a document, it is 7d, whatever the documented episode; (ii) if a process rule was not followed, it is 9, even when it produced an error of another type (which becomes secondary); (iii) if a control flagged where it should not, or stayed silent where it should have flagged, it is 8, even when the cause is environmental (10 secondary); (iv) if the test influences a training or selection choice, it is 1, not 5; 5 covers budgets and selections made without test information; (v) if a wrong alignment causes data to be discarded, it is 2 with 3 secondary; 3 is primary only when the alignment is correct and the loss comes from a filter or fallback; (vi) 4c prevails over 4b when the problem exists only in the comparison across configurations; (vii) 6 only for the text of the manuscript; a wrong sentence in a record is 7. Boundary examples, noted in the minutes, extend the list without changing the rules.

**Revision of the taxonomy.** If «Other» exceeds 10% of the problems, the first version is kept with its complete coding; the new version is declared and the **whole corpus** re-coded with it; the paper reports both phases.

## 6. Choice of incidents (RQ1)

A **reasoned and explicit selection, not independent of the outcomes**. Criteria, in this order: (1) coverage of different types and first detectors; (2) at least one incident of type 8a; (3) at least one incident that contradicts a lesson proposed by another, or a problem left unresolved, if one exists in the corpus; (4) at least one incident per paper; (5) reconstructibility: the four traces exist or their absence is declared. Incidents are chosen after the corpus is frozen (§8, phase 4), among the classified problems, with a written justification. I1–I3, reconstructed before this document, enter only if they meet the criteria, assessed then. Every card has the nine sections of I1–I3, the fields of §5.1, the evidence status per row, and the question «which other explanation is compatible with the traces, and what is missing to choose».

## 7. Verifiable statements (formulated with the knowledge of §2)

Admissible outcomes for each: **refuted** (a documented counter-example), **supported in the examined corpus**, **not assessable** (missing sources, unknown detectors, or no case to test it on). Fractions with numerator and denominator, no percentages.

- **S1.** In every incident reconstructed **after signature**, the search of §7.1 finds at least one 7d (documentary) problem in the register or the protocol. *Refuted if* for at least one incident the completed search finds none. *Not assessable if* no incident is reconstructed after signature. Note: observed on I2 and I3 and on item 33; not on I1; for this reason I1–I3 are excluded.
  - **7.1 Search procedure and stopping rule**: for each incident, the date, time, author, machine and cause declared in each register item and in the protocol are compared with the timestamps and messages of the cited commits and with the cited artefacts; the search stops when every declared field has a match (concordant or discordant) or is marked «no artefact».
- **S2.** No problem of type **4b** (inadequate metric implemented faithfully) or **6** has a mechanical control as its first observed detector. *Refuted if* at least one has. Type 4a is excluded because a declared/implemented discrepancy may be detectable automatically.
- **S3.** In the Paper 4 corpus, the problems with phase of introduction «deliverable» are at least as many as those with phase «regeneration», counting shared problems once, in the paper where they were first registered. *Refuted if* they are fewer. *Not assessable if* more than a quarter of the Paper 4 problems have phase «unknown».

The former statement on defective controls («discovered by a condition the control did not foresee») is dropped because «not foreseen» cannot be defined without circularity; how type-8 problems were discovered is described in RQ2.

## 8. Procedure

**Phase 1 — Inventory.** The coordination role compiles `INVENTORY.md` (§3): every file and every git search with path, commit, fingerprint, and the number of candidate items it yields. Frozen with fingerprint.

**Phase 2 — Extraction.** The script `protocol/processo/analisi/extract_candidates.py` produces the list of candidate items from registers, deviations, git search and flags in the reports, with no type and no phase. Output frozen with fingerprint.

**Phase 3 — Separation and deduplication.** The two human co-author coders, **COD-01** and **COD-02** (from the internal-review role; identities assigned in a separate confidential allocation note), together, decide for each candidate item how many problems it contains, which items are duplicates or shared, and the status (§4); every decision is written in the minutes `protocol/processo/corpus/MINUTES_PHASE3.md`. No type and no phase are assigned here.

**Phase 4 — Freezing the corpus.** `protocol/processo/corpus/CORPUS.json` with IDs, references and status; fingerprint; commit. From here the denominator does not change: problems discovered later (including by P2, by S1 or by the reconstruction of new incidents) go into `CORPUS_POST.json`, counted and reported separately.

**Phase 5 — Independent coding.** The two coders assign the fields of §5.1 without seeing each other; Cohen's κ for primary type, phase, stage and first detector; disagreements resolved by discussion with minutes; the coding before discussion is kept.

**Phase 6 — External verifier** (human, outside the group and the consortium): **EXT-01** (Kiranet; see §10.1 for the codes). Their previous involvement with OR3, if any, and any relevant relationship are recorded in the confidential register of §11. Public reporting describes relevant involvement and relationships without disclosing personal names. EXT-01 completes and freezes all phase-6 outputs before receiving any P2 reports for the separate adjudication task in §10. Receives (a) a random sample (declared seed) of at least 30% of the problems, **anonymised** (description of the problem and artefact, without stage, detector, roles, phase), and assigns **primary type and status**; (b) for the same sample, a **separate provenance package** (commits, dates, records) without the coders' codings, and assigns **phase of introduction**; external κ reported separately for (a) and (b). In addition, verifies on the original evidence the reconstruction of at least one incident of their choice and writes a dated addendum.

**Phase 7 — Analysis.** `protocol/processo/analisi/main.py` produces every table and every number of the paper from `CORPUS.json`, `CORPUS_POST.json`, the cards and the session logs; the paper contains no number that does not come from there.
