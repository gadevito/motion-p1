## Question

The deliverable concluded that, in the severe regime, "having neighbours matters, not which":

- on 12 compact sub-networks with spacing between 42 and 396 m, the propagation advantage showed no relation
  to spacing;
- in two configurations, a randomised adjacency matched the kNN graph.

None of those numbers can be cited (PROTOCOLLO §1.1):

- the randomisation preserved only the edge count;
- hidden nodes entered the loss;
- windows crossed calendar jumps;
- the negative control was run on 3 points, two of them chosen after seeing the curve, and could be
  regenerated only after fixing the pairing in the conserved script (RC 4).

E11 asks the same question under protocol. **Does the given kNN graph give a larger propagation advantage than
a randomised graph with the same degree sequence, at each of the 12 spacings?** It also asks whether the
advantage V depends on spacing.

The deliverable's six excluded configurations (connectivity 0.505–0.929) and the 594/297/99 series stay out, as
the deliverable itself declared that series not applicable.

**The kNN graph is undirected.** `make_geo_sweep.py` builds an `nx.Graph` and adds an edge from each node to
its k = 4 nearest neighbours, so the graph is the symmetrised union of the k-nearest relations (mean degree
5.1–5.8, not 4). After the 20 m alignment, sensors that coincide are merged. Any self-loop this creates is
removed and counted; the deliverable's kernel already ignored self-loops (`gomo_sparse.py:150`, diagonal set to
zero). The resulting simple graph is G1's adjacency.

**Weights of the randomised graphs.** Both randomised graphs receive exactly the multiset of G1's edge
lengths, permuted over their edges, so the kernel input, σ, the sum and the distribution of the weights are
identical to G1. The geographic length of the new edges is computed and recorded, but it does not enter the
kernel. The control therefore changes the topology only.

This departs from the deliverable on purpose. There, the randomised edges carried their new geographic
distance (`make_geo_sweep.py:49`, `weight = D[a, b] + 1`) and a σ of their own. The kernel distribution then
differed between the true and the randomised graph: on the three preserved randomised graphs, mean kernel
0.23–0.36 against 0.20–0.48 for the kNN graphs, with a much more skewed distribution. Those randomised graphs
were also not connected. Both points are recorded in the register of corrections.

**A new randomised graph is drawn for every seed**, so that the variability of the randomisation enters the
interval; the deliverable used one graph with seed 0. Each run records, for each randomised graph:

- the degree sequence;
- the multiset of weights and its fingerprint;
- the mean geographic edge length;
- connectivity;
- the number of swaps performed or draws rejected.

**Random streams.** Every random choice has its own generator, `numpy.random.default_rng([seed, k])`, with a
fixed *k* per stream:

| *k* | Stream |
|---|---|
| 1 | node sets (cell shuffling) |
| 2 | per-batch masks (share *u* and choice of the input cells) |
| 3 | batch order |
| 4 | graph randomisation (topology) |
| 5 | permutation of the weights over the randomised edges |
| 6 | initialisation of the network weights (seed passed to `torch.Generator`) |

Changing or adding an arm therefore leaves the node sets, the masks and the batch order of a seed unchanged:
all arms of a seed see the same sequence of batches and masks. Any residual non-determinism of the MPS device is
measured by the preflight (two identical runs of one arm) and declared.

## Node sets, masks and training

**Grid.** Each sub-network uses the smallest grid G × G with G ≥ 6 that has at least 20 occupied cells, on
the extent of its nodes' UTM coordinates. Madrid stays at 6×6 in six configurations out of eight (7×7 in
`comp_q5050` and `comp_q8020`); Melbourne goes from 7×7 to 17×17.

**Node sets per seed.** The occupied cells are taken in the order of stream *k* = 1 and assigned whole. Each
cell goes to the set with the largest deficit with respect to the target shares, 40% observed, 20%
validation-hidden and 40% test-hidden (deficit = target share × N − nodes already assigned). The realised
shares are recorded. The same three sets are used by all four arms of a seed (fingerprint checked).

The grid and the assignment rule were fixed by the internal review on 2026-11-06 (16:30), before any pilot.
They replace the rule "6×6 grid, observed until ≥ 40% then validation until ≥ 20%". The preflight of
2026-11-06 found that rule inapplicable: the sub-networks have 7–25 occupied 6×6 cells, with up to 31 nodes
per cell, so the validation-hidden set was empty in 3 of the 60 (configuration, seed) pairs.

**Geometry covariates, registered for every configuration and seed:**

- the grid G and the number of occupied cells;
- nodes per cell (minimum, median, maximum);
- the cell side in metres, √(side_x · side_y);
- for each test-hidden node, the UTM distance to the nearest observed node (median and interquartile range
  per configuration).

The cell side and the median distance enter the E11 tables next to the spacing.

**Masks in training (D1).** In each batch, a share *u* ~ U(0.6, 0.95) of the observed cells is input; the
remaining observed nodes are loss targets. The share is drawn in whole cells. Hidden nodes are never input and
never targets. At evaluation all observed nodes are input. The resulting shift between training and evaluation
(60–95% of the observed set in training, 100% at evaluation) is declared here and in the paper. Decided by the
internal review on 2026-11-06; E9 inherits the same rule at every *f*.

**Origins and split.** Admissible origins only (15-min cadence, PROTOCOLLO §2.3; register of corrections
item 1). Temporal split 70 / 10 / 20 of admissible origins.

**Training.** A fixed number of updates *U*, no early stopping, 11 checkpoints evenly spaced. The checkpoint is
selected by the minimum wCE on validation-hidden nodes in the validation period. Normalisation and class
weights come from training-period cells of observed nodes. **No static attribute:** `max_speed` was removed
after the diagnostic pilot, where it acted as a node identifier (D1 below); its input channel is kept at zero.
lr 1e-3, no dropout. Architecture as in PROTOCOLLO §2.3. **Budget: U = 1,000 updates**, 11 checkpoints every
≈ 91 updates (from the pilot, below).

**Pilot for *U*, before the signature (validation only).**

Every run of the pilot used Madrid `comp_q5050`, seed 0, 7×7 grid (35 / 26 / 34 nodes observed /
validation-hidden / test-hidden), validation every 250 updates, and no test metric.

The rule is the same for every pilot run:

- *U* is the first checkpoint at which validation wCE is within 1% of its minimum, for both arms, rounded

- adequacy requires the same order of G0 and G1 at *U* and at 2*U*; if the order differs, *U* doubles once.

## Unit of analysis and numbers

- Cell = (configuration, seed, corridor, node).
- 12 configurations × 5 seeds (0–4) × 4 arms = **240 trainings**.
- Test-hidden nodes per configuration ≈ 32–40.

V and the differences between arms are paired by seed. Intervals come from a joint resample of seeds × corridor
blocks (test-hidden nodes inside the block), multiplicities preserved, 4,000 replicates, 95% percentiles,
bootstrap seed **20261106**. The same replicate indices are used for every arm of a configuration.

**Limit, declared here and in the paper.** The blocks are the grid cells. With about 20 occupied cells per
sub-network, the test-hidden nodes fall in about 8 blocks per configuration and seed, so the intervals will be
wide. The frozen record reports the median half-width of I_c; the paper states this limit next to the
verdicts.

## Criteria, with thresholds

### Quantities, unit and direction (stated once)

For configuration *c*, seed *s* and arm *a*, let wCE_{c,s}(a) be the weighted cross-entropy on test-hidden nodes,
mean over admissible test origins and the four horizons.

- **Propagation advantage:** V_c(a) = mean over *s* of 100 · (wCE_{c,s}(G0) − wCE_{c,s}(a)) / wCE_{c,s}(G0).
- **Unit:** percentage points of the wCE of G0, the no-graph reference.
- **Direction:** positive means arm *a* has a lower wCE than G0, that is, it is better.
- **Difference between two arms:** D_c(a, b) = V_c(a) − V_c(b) = mean over *s* of
  100 · (wCE_{c,s}(b) − wCE_{c,s}(a)) / wCE_{c,s}(G0). It is a relative wCE difference normalised on G0, and it
  is **positive when *a* is better than *b***.

Every interval I is the 95% percentile interval of the resample described above.

### Two reading scales, as in Paper 1

Every difference D with interval I = [L, U] is read on two scales. The verdicts below are derived only from
them.

| Scale | Reading | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| sign | **favourable** to *a* | L > 0 |
| sign | **unfavourable** to *a* | U < 0 |
| sign | **inconclusive** | L ≤ 0 ≤ U |
| margin | **an advantage of *a* of 3 points or more is excluded** | U < +3 |
| margin | **an effect of 3 points or more in either direction is excluded** | −3 < L and U < +3 |
| margin | **reached** | L ≥ +3 |
| margin | **not excluded** | every other case |

**Where the margin of 3 points comes from.** A difference of 3 points of the G0 wCE is a relative wCE
difference of 3%. That is the equivalence margin, ±3% relative, that Paper 1 (§12) inherited from the
deliverable and uses as a declared convention. It is also the "design threshold" of 3.00 points that the
deliverable's own geometry protocol set for a relevant spacing effect (deliverable, Table 33, criterion 1).
It is a convention, not the result of a power calculation, and it is declared as such. Before the signature,
E11 does not estimate how often it can be reached: the pilot is read only for its assertions. After the
campaign, the frozen record reports the median half-width of I_c, so the reader can see how far the 12
intervals could discriminate at that margin.

### Criterion 1 — negative control (primary)

D_c = D_c(G1, G1-rand(deg)), with interval I_c = [L_c, U_c]. Each configuration gets exactly one verdict:

| Verdict | Derived from | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| **proximity prevails** | sign favourable and D_c ≥ 3 | L_c > 0 and D_c ≥ +3 |
| **geometry advantage ≥ 3 points excluded** | margin | U_c < +3; sub-labelled "randomised ≥ real" when the sign is unfavourable or D_c ≤ 0 |
| **not discriminating** | — | every other case |

The summary statement for the paper is derived mechanically from the verdicts:

| Statement | Condition |
|---|---|
| **"geometry matters"** | "prevails" in at least 2 configurations of the same city |
| **"no evidence that geometry matters in 42–396 m"** | "excluded" in at least 10 of the 12 configurations, and "prevails" in none |
| **"undetermined"** | every other case, reported with the three counts |

**Multiple comparisons.** There is no correction for the 12 comparisons. The protection against a single false
positive is the 3-point threshold together with the two-configuration requirement in one city. This is declared
here and in the paper.

### Secondary comparison — does the graph beat no graph?

V_c(G1) = D_c(G1, G0), with its interval and its two readings, for each configuration. Also reported:
V_c(G1-rand(deg)) and V_c(G1-rand(edges)).

If V_c(G1) is not favourable in any configuration, the paper says from these numbers that the given graph does
not beat no graph in E11. The question "which geometry" is then empty, and criterion 1 is reported but not read
as an answer to it. G1-rand(edges) stays secondary, for comparability with the deliverable:
D_c(G1, G1-rand(edges)) is reported with both readings and no verdict.

### Criterion 2 — trend (secondary)

OLS of V_c(G1) on the realised spacing with an additive city term. The slope
interval comes from the same replicates: V_c is recomputed per replicate and the model refitted. The trend is
supported only if the slope interval excludes zero **and** the fitted difference over the observed spacing range
is at least 3 points. Because spacing and city are nearly confounded (Madrid 42–88 m, Melbourne 95–396 m), the
simple regression without the city term is reported next to it, never in place of it.

**Declared sensitivity analysis (not a criterion).** The same regression with the city term is repeated with
the median distance from test-hidden to the nearest observed node in place of the spacing, and the result is
reported next to criterion 2. It serves to say whether a "geometry matters" verdict is a matter of distance
from the nearest sensor or of topology.

### Criterion 3 — threshold form (secondary)

Three models of V_c(G1) on spacing, each with the city term:

- constant;
- linear;
- two lines with a free breakpoint (at least 3 configurations on each side).

They are compared by AIC. The threshold form wins only if both conditions hold:

- its AIC is lower than both others by at least 2;
- the interval of the **difference** between the two slopes excludes zero.

The breakpoint is reported with its bootstrap interval. The range 130–380 m has no configuration, so a breakpoint
there is declared not identifiable. This is the deliverable's criterion 5 as its protocol wrote it. The paper
also reports how the deliverable's implementation differed from it: a residual-sum rule in place of AIC, each
slope tested against zero, and no interval on the breakpoint.

### Reported without a criterion

- the difference between the two randomisations, D_c(G1-rand(deg), G1-rand(edges));
- the cost of not having the sensor (wCE of G1 on test-hidden − wCE of G1 on observed nodes, same run);
- red recall at 5% false alarms with the threshold chosen on validation-hidden nodes;
- every quantity per horizon, in an appendix.

## Mandatory controls

Preflight, on one configuration and one seed, before the campaign. Each line is an assertion:

1. The three node sets are disjoint and are unions of whole cells. No validation-hidden or test-hidden index
   appears in the input tensor or the loss tensor of any batch.
2. Every origin is admissible (cadence from the first input step to the last target). Causality: noise on
   every value after the origin leaves inputs, normalisation and class weights identical to the bit.
3. Normalisation and class weights are computed from training-period cells of observed nodes only; the test
   recomputes them independently and compares.
4. N after alignment equals the real-N column of the Design table for every configuration.
4-bis. In all 12 configurations × 5 seeds no node set is empty and every realised share is within ±15 points
   of 40/20/40. The geometry covariates are written for every pair.
5. G1-rand(deg) has the same degree sequence as G1, node by node. G1-rand(edges) has the same number of edges.
   Both randomised graphs are connected and simple. The number of swaps performed, or of draws rejected, is
   logged.

   5-bis. The weights of each randomised graph are a permutation of G1's: sorted multisets identical, sums equal
   to the bit, σ identical. The geographic lengths of the new edges are recorded and do not enter the kernel.
6. The node sets, admissible origins and class weights of a seed have identical fingerprints across the four
   arms.
7. The number of optimiser updates equals *U* within 1%. The validation set evaluated has the size of the
   calendar (all admissible validation origins × validation-hidden nodes).
8. The per-cell prediction file exists for each run and reconstructs the aggregate wCE within 1e-9.
9. Each run writes to a folder named by the fingerprint of its full configuration; an existing name blocks the
   launch.

## What invalidates the experiment

- the configuration list, the thresholds or the bootstrap seed changed after any test number;
- a seed removed or replaced;
- selection on test-hidden nodes;
- any hidden node in input or loss;
- G1-rand(deg) not preserving the degree sequence, or any randomised graph not connected;
- a randomised graph whose weights are not a permutation of G1's;
- random streams shared between node sets, masks and graph randomisation;
- pilot runs reused in the campaign;
- node sets differing between arms of the same seed;
- arms of one configuration run on different machines or devices;
- per-cell predictions missing;
- a number of the frozen record written by hand.

## What changes in the paper

**Before the E11 results, the paper has a paragraph on the inductive regime and on the positive control.** On
nodes never seen, the best validation wCE in the pilot is about 3% below ln 3, and G1 is about equal to G0. With
the hidden nodes as training targets (D2-bis, the deliverable's condition) the same pipeline goes to 0.94. The
reader thus knows the scale on which V is read, and the size of the deliverable's defect D1.


- **"Geometry matters"** gives a new result against the deliverable: which spacing, how much, and whether the
  cruder edge-count randomisation hides it. E9 criterion 3 then becomes the test of whether this holds when
  coverage drops.
- **"No evidence"** means the deliverable's conclusion stands for the first time with a degree-preserving
  control, 12 points and intervals. The paper then says that, for these models, the geography of the
  neighbours does not matter in 42–396 m. It adds "connecting neighbours matters" only where the secondary
  comparison V_c(G1) is favourable.
- **"Undetermined"** is reported with the counts. The paper does not claim either.

In every case the deliverable's numbers appear only in the appendix of its defects.
