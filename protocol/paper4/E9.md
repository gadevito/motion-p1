## Question

How does the forecast on uninstrumented segments change with the instrumented fraction *f*, and is there a
coverage below which propagation from instrumented neighbours stops helping? The deliverable evaluated one
point (≈ 44%) and declared that its data could not locate a threshold. E11 found, on compact sub-networks, no
measurable gain of the given graph over no graph (V(G1) favourable in 0 of 12 configurations). E9 asks whether
that holds on whole cities and at every coverage. It also asks how far the forecast on hidden nodes is from
the uniform predictor (ln 3) and from the no-graph model: the absolute numbers an operator needs.

## Design

| Factor | Levels | Note |
|---|---|---|
| City | Madrid (828 nodes), London (774), Melbourne (587) | whole cities, Traffic4cast; population after the 20 m alignment (RC 3) |
| Observed fraction *f* | 10, 20, 40, 60% | brief §9b; the realised share is recorded and used as abscissa |
| Seed | 0–4 | the seed determines the node sets (stream *k* = 1); five seeds from the power calculation below |
| Arm | G0 (no graph), G1 (road graph), G1-rand(deg) | brief §9b, D2 |

**180 trainings** (4 levels × 5 seeds × 3 arms × 3 cities).

**Given graph.** The road graph of each city (`t4c*_routes-graph.pkl`), aligned as in E11. After alignment it
is connected in all three cities: one component of 828, 774 and 587 nodes. It is used as an undirected simple
graph; the self-loops created by merged sensors are removed and counted (72, 126 and 7). The edge weight is
the Gaussian kernel on edge length with σ = standard deviation of the graph's own lengths, as in E11.

**G1-rand(deg).** `networkx.connected_double_edge_swap` with 10·|E| swaps requested, seed of the run (stream
*k* = 4). The graph receives G1's weight multiset permuted over its edges (stream *k* = 5). Degree sequence,
connectivity and weights are identical to G1 (preflight 5 and 5-bis, as in E11). The deliverable's edge-count
randomisation is not repeated: it was secondary in E11, which reported it.

**Grid.** Per city, the smallest grid G × G with G ≥ 6 such that, for every *f* and every seed:

- no node set is empty;
- every realised share is within ±15 points of its target;
- the test-hidden nodes fall in **at least 8 blocks**.

The grid is computed from the node coordinates and the node-set rule only, before any training: **G = 11 for
Madrid and London, G = 15 for Melbourne**, with 45, 46 and 53 occupied cells.

*Approved by the internal review on 2026-11-07*, in place of the rule first written at 22:15 on 2026-11-06
(smallest G ≥ 6 with at least 40 occupied cells). That rule gives G = 10, 10 and 12 with 45, 43 and 40 occupied cells, but at *f* =
60% it leaves 5 of the 60 (city, *f*, seed) pairs with fewer than 8 test blocks (5–6), so the preflight
assertion it was meant to secure would fail (`e9_potenza_g40.json`, d5a0d5e2bf1532bf). The proposed rule
secures the assertion directly (`e9_potenza_b8.json`, cb57a5e611adee68).

**Node sets.** The occupied cells are taken in the order of stream *k* = 1 and assigned whole, by deficit on
the target shares:

| Set | Target share |
|---|---|
| observed | *f* |
| validation-hidden | 20% |

**Perimeter, unchanged from the signed E11:**

- admissible origins, split 70/10/20 with purge;

- 11 checkpoints, selection on validation-hidden nodes.

## Power calculation (before the signature, reviews 20:45 and 22:15)

`paper4/code/e9_potenza.py --min-test-blocks 8` (`paper4/runs/e9_potenza_b8.json`, cb57a5e611adee68) takes
the dispersion of V(G1) − V(G0) from the 12 E11 configurations, test-hidden nodes:

- between seeds: median variance 5.13;
- within a seed, from a block resample: median per-block unit 1.82.

The expected half-width with *n_s* seeds and *n_b* test blocks is 1.96·√(σ²_seed/*n_s* + *u*/(*n_b*·*n_s*)).
*n_b* is the smallest count over the five seeds of the E9 node sets on the proposed grid.

| *f* | Test blocks (min over seeds): Madrid / London / Melbourne | Half-width, 5 seeds | 8 seeds | 12 seeds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10% | 27 / 27 / 33 | 2.00 | 1.58 | 1.29 |
| 20% | 25 / 24 / 29 | 2.00 | 1.58 | 1.29 |
| 40% | 18 / 15 / 16 | 2.00–2.01 | 1.58–1.59 | 1.29–1.30 |
| 60% | 8 / 9 / 8 | 2.02–2.03 | 1.60 | 1.31 |

Half-widths are in points of the G0 wCE, unscaled (conservative); scaling the block variance by block size
changes them by less than 0.05. The seed variance dominates.

**Rule, written before the launch.** If the expected half-width exceeded 3 points at any *f*, the seeds would
go up to the smallest number that brings it below 3 at every *f*. With 5 seeds it is ≤ 2.03 at every *f*, so
**5 seeds are kept**.

**Limit.** The between-seed variance comes from compact sub-networks of 81–99 nodes. On whole cities it may be
smaller; the projection is an order of magnitude. The frozen record reports the realised half-widths.

## Unit of analysis and resample

- Cell = (city, *f*, seed, corridor, node).
- wCE_{c,f,s}(a) on test-hidden nodes: per horizon, Σ W[y]·(−log p_y) / Σ W[y] over valid cells, then mean
  over the four horizons.
- V_{c,f}(a) = mean over seeds of 100·(wCE(G0) − wCE(a))/wCE(G0), in points of the G0 wCE, **positive when *a*
  is better than G0**; D(a, b) = V(a) − V(b), positive when *a* is better than *b*.
- Intervals: 4,000 replicates, seeds resampled with replacement and, inside each drawn seed, the corridor blocks
  (grid cells) holding test-hidden nodes, multiplicities kept; the same replicate indices for every arm of a (city, *f*);
  95% percentiles; generator `default_rng([20261106, index of (city, f)])`.
- Cells without a valid target are excluded with their count; nodes without any valid target are excluded
  with their count.
- Two reading scales, as in E11: the sign (favourable / unfavourable / inconclusive) and the margin of 3
  points (reached / excluded). The margin has the same provenance as in E11: ±3% relative, the convention of
  Paper 1 §12 and of the deliverable.

## Criteria, with thresholds

**(a) Propagation at *f* (primary).** For each city and *f*: **"propagation helps at *f*"** if V_{c,f}(G1) is
favourable (L > 0) and V_{c,f}(G1) ≥ 3 points. The curve V(f) is reported for each city with its intervals.

**(b) Absolute level (primary, reported for every *f*).** wCE_{c,f}(G1) and wCE_{c,f}(G0) on test-hidden nodes
with intervals, and their distance from ln 3 = 1.0986: 100·(ln 3 − wCE)/ln 3, with interval. These are the
numbers the operator reads. No threshold: they are reported whatever their value.

**(c) Coverage threshold (secondary).** On V(G1) at the 12 points (3 cities × 4 levels), with a city term,
three models are compared by AIC:

- constant;
- linear in *f*;
- a step at *f** between two consecutive levels: V = a + city + b·1[*f* > *f**], with *f** ∈ {15, 30, 50%}.

The step form wins only if both conditions hold:

- its AIC is lower than both others by at least 2;
- the interval of the step size *b* excludes zero.

*f** is reported with its bootstrap distribution. This is criterion 3 of E11, adapted to four levels.

**(d) Geometry at *f* (secondary).** D_{c,f} = D(G1, G1-rand(deg)) with interval, and the verdict of E11
criterion 1 for each city and *f*:

| Verdict | Condition |
|---|---|
| proximity prevails | L > 0 and D ≥ 3 |
| geometry advantage ≥ 3 points excluded | U < 3 |
| not discriminating | every other case |

**Summary statement, derived mechanically from (a).** Let H(*f*) be the number of cities in which "propagation
helps at *f*".

| Statement | Condition |
|---|---|
| **"propagation helps above *f**"** | *f** is the smallest level such that H(*f*′) ≥ 2 for every *f*′ ≥ *f**; if *f** = 10%, the statement is "propagation helps at every *f* ≤ 60%" |
| **"no measurable propagation at any *f* ≤ 60%"** | at every *f* and in every city, V(G1) has U < 3 (an advantage of 3 points or more excluded), and (a) never holds |
| **"undetermined"** | every other case, reported with H(*f*) for each *f* and the number of (city, *f*) pairs where the margin is not excluded |

There is no correction for the 12 (city, *f*) comparisons. The 3-point threshold and the two-city requirement
are the protection, declared here and in the paper.

**Reported without a criterion:**

- red recall on test-hidden nodes at 5% false alarms (threshold chosen on validation-hidden nodes), for each arm
  and *f*, next to the false-alarm rate itself (in E11, recall was close to the false-alarm rate in Madrid);
- wCE of G1 on the observed nodes of the same run and period, with the caveat of E11 (targets never have their
  own input in training);
- every quantity per horizon, in an appendix.

## Mandatory controls

The E11 preflight (assertions 1–9, 4-bis, 5, 5-bis, determinism, guards), run on Madrid at *f* = 40% and on
every (city, *f*) for the node-set assertions, plus:

- for each city, the road graph after alignment is connected, and the number of self-loops removed is logged;
- for each (city, *f*, seed): no empty set, every realised share within ±15 points, **at least 8 test blocks**;
- the grid of each city equals the one computed by the rule (G = 11, 11, 15), and the covariates (cell side,
  median distance test-hidden → observed) are written for every pair;
- U and the 11 checkpoints are identical for every *f*.

## What invalidates the experiment

Everything listed in E11, plus:

- a level of *f* added, removed or changed after any test number;
- *U* chosen per city or per *f*;
- the seeds reduced, or raised after the launch;
- G1 not the aligned road graph;
- a grid other than the one given by the rule.

## Cost

180 trainings of 1,000 updates.

**Isolated measurement, 2026-11-07:** Madrid at *f* = 40%, seed 0, one process, two repetitions with the arms in
the order G0, G1, G1-rand(deg).

| Arm | Repetition 1 | Repetition 2 |
|---|---|---|
| G0 | 90.8 s | 87.1 s |
| G1 | 179.1 s | 159.4 s |
| G1-rand(deg) | 303.8 s | 393.9 s |

G1-rand(deg) has the same architecture and the same dense adjacency size as G1. A diagnostic of 50 updates,
alternating G1 and G1-rand(deg) in one process (not a cost measurement), gave equal times: 10.2–14.1 s against
9.9–12.2 s. The slower third run is therefore attributed to the machine state during the measurement, not to the
arm: load averages of 36–62 were recorded at its end. This is declared. The cost table uses G0 ≈ 89 s and G1 ≈
G1-rand(deg) ≈ 169 s on Madrid, the largest city; London (774 nodes) and Melbourne (587) cost at most as much.

**E9 campaign ≈ 7 h of process time at most** (60 (city, *f*, seed) cells × (89 + 169 + 169) s). The upper bound
from the raw measurements, with the slow third run, is 10.7 h.

Previous estimate, kept for comparison: from the E11 cost unit (142.5 s for G1 on Madrid at 1,000 updates),
≈ 6 h of process time if *U* = 1,000. Before the signature, besides the pilot, **one isolated run per arm** (G0, G1,
G1-rand(deg)) on Madrid at *f* = 40%, seed 0, *U* updates, gives the cost table. Those runs are not reused in
the campaign. Calendar
time may use concurrent processes; the cost table uses only isolated times.

## What changes in the paper

- **"Propagation helps above *f**"** locates the coverage at which instrumented neighbours start to pay, with
  the absolute wCE at each *f*: the number for "how many sensors".
- **"No measurable propagation at any *f* ≤ 60%"** means E11's result extends to whole cities and to every
  coverage. In this inductive regime, the value of a sensor lies in its own segment, and the paper says so with
  the absolute distance from ln 3.
- **"Undetermined"** is reported with the counts; the paper does not claim either.

In every case, criterion (b) gives the operator the absolute numbers, and the E9 paragraph refers to the
inductive regime and to the positive control of E11 (D2-bis).
