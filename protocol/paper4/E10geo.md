## Question

E12 found that the labels carry a spatial signal only within about 250 m of an instrumented segment. E10-geo
asks how much the placement rule changes the share of uncovered segments within that radius, at the same
number of sensors. That is the only way placement could matter, given E9 and E12.

## Design

**Cities and segments.** Madrid, London and Melbourne, with the populations of E9 (828, 774, 587 segments after
the 20 m alignment). UTM coordinates of the segments come from the sensor tables.

**Coverage.** *f* ∈ {10, 20, 40, 60}%: round(*f*·K) instrumented segments; every other segment is "uncovered".

**Placement rules**, each drawn 20 times per (city, *f*):

| Rule | How the instrumented segments are chosen |
|---|---|
| **Blocks** (E9) | the observed set of the E9 node-set rule: whole grid cells (11×11, 11×11, 15×15), assigned by deficit on (*f*, 20%, 80% − *f*); draw *k* uses seed *k* = 0…19 of stream *k* = 1. The observed share is the realised one (within ±15 points of *f*); the other two rules use exactly the same number of instrumented segments as this rule in the same draw |
| **Uniform** | that number of segments drawn uniformly without replacement |
| **Density-stratified** | segments split into five strata by local density (number of segments within 500 m, quintiles); the same number of instrumented segments allocated equally across the five strata (remainder to the densest strata), drawn uniformly within each stratum. This oversamples sparse areas |

Random generators: `numpy.random.default_rng([20261108, rule index, city index, level index, draw])`, with rule
index 1 (uniform) and 2 (density-stratified), city and level indices in the order of E9. The blocks rule uses the
E9 node-set rule with seed = draw. If a stratum holds fewer segments than its allocation, it is filled
completely and the excess goes to the next densest stratum.

## Quantities (for every city, *f*, rule; mean and 2.5–97.5% range over the 20 draws)

- share of uncovered segments with an instrumented segment within 250 m;
- the same within 500 m;
- median distance from an uncovered segment to the nearest instrumented one;
- the realised instrumented share.

**Reported as a table and as a curve:** share within 250 m against *f*, one line per rule and city.

No criterion. The table says how close placement can bring uncovered segments to the radius of the signal.
E12-bis says whether that radius is worth anything in wCE.

## Mandatory controls (preflight)

1. The populations and coordinates are those of E9 (fingerprints of the aligned node lists).
2. The blocks rule reproduces the E9 observed sets for draws 0–4 (identical to the E9 run records).
3. In every draw the three rules instrument the same number of segments, and no segment twice.
4. Determinism: two runs with the same generators give identical tables.

## What invalidates it

- a rule, the radii, the strata or the number of draws changed after the table is computed;
- a population different from E9's.

## Cost

Seconds per (city, *f*); no GPU.

## What changes in the paper

A table and a curve in the "where" section. If uniform or density-stratified placement brings many more uncovered
segments within 250 m than blocks do, the paper says so, together with what E12-bis says that proximity is
worth. If the rules differ little, placement cannot change the share of uncovered segments near a sensor, and
the "where" question closes on geometry alone.
