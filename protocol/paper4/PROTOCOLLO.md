## 0. Method standard inherited from Paper 1

These rules apply to both parts unchanged.

1. A pre-registration per experiment (`protocol/paper4/E<n>.md`; `protocol/paper4/P4A.md` for Part A). It
   states the question, design, arms, criterion with a numerical threshold, mandatory controls, what
   invalidates it, what changes in the paper, and costs. It is saved with fingerprint and signed before the
   first run. Changes after results go to a "deviations" section.
2. Frozen records `CONGELAMENTO_<name>.md` are generated by script from the run files, with the fingerprints of
   every input, and never written by hand. Corrections are made by regenerating or by a dated erratum appended
   at the end.
3. Per-cell predictions (node × origin × horizon × class) are saved for every run (Part B). Every number of the
   paper is recomputable by one script, `paper4/analysis/main.py`, which prints the input fingerprints.
4. Every reported interval comes from a resample. Results are read on two scales, as in Paper 1 §III-B: the
   sign of the interval (favourable / unfavourable / inconclusive) and the margin (whether an effect of the
   declared size is reached or excluded). The margin is ±3% relative, declared as a convention. Each
   E<n>.md states the unit, the direction and the derivation of its verdicts from the two scales.
5. Selection (thresholds, hyper-parameters, checkpoints) is made on validation only and written to a
   fingerprinted file before any test metric is computed.
6. Preflight assertions run before every campaign. A failing test is fixed, not commented.
7. Two registers are kept up to date: the register of choices (value, provenance: discussed / inherited /
   imposed by data) and the register of corrections. One tag per verified version, created by the author.
8. Costs are measured in an isolated session on the Mac M3 (one process, idle machine). Calendar time may use
   several processes in parallel, but times measured under concurrency never enter the cost table. If another
   machine becomes available, all arms of one comparison stay on the same machine and device.

## 1. Thesis and questions

### 1.1 Thesis

*Note of 2026-11-08 (internal review, 14:30).* Part B is closed (E11, E9, E12, E12-bis, E10-geo, E12-ter; frozen
records 4e04ba7, 1d97a94, 085bda7, 40adfc9, 85cd9e8, dda3b8f). The thesis is rewritten on its results:

> **On segments without a sensor, these data carry no forecastable spatial information beyond the calendar at
> 15–120 min; what an operator buys with a sensor is the measurement at that segment, and the choice of what to
> measure (Part A).**

What supports each part of it:

- *No gain from propagation.* The learned graph model gains nothing over the model without spatial exchange
  on compact sub-networks at 43–397 m spacing (E11) or on whole cities at 10–60% coverage (E9).
- *A short signal in the labels.* Nearby labels agree above chance only within about 250 m (E12).
- *Not usable as it is.* Calibrated on the instrumented segments, that agreement does not beat the model
  without spatial exchange (E12-bis).
- *Shared calendar.* A table of the hour of the week alone forecasts at least as well as that model. Net of the
  calendar, the nearest neighbour's class is worth nothing within 250 m and less than nothing beyond (E12-ter).
- *Placement.* It decides how many uncovered segments lie within 250 m of a sensor (E10-geo). Given the above,
  it does not decide how well they are forecast.
- *What to measure.* The second clause rests on Part A, which is still to be run (§1.3).

No E13 is added. The value of the neighbour net of the calendar is measured as zero without a model. A second
learned model would have to extract from the neighbours a signal that the calendar does not carry. Neither
the learned propagation (E9, E11) nor the calibrated tables (E12-bis, E12-ter) found one. The paper argues
this in its discussion.

**Thesis as drafted on 2026-11-06, superseded.** It is kept for the record, with what became of each part:

> **For congestion classification on road segments that have no sensor, what an operator can buy is coverage
> and the right measurement, not a clever placement.**
>
> - Above a minimum fraction of instrumented network, propagation from instrumented neighbours adds a bounded
>   gain on the uninstrumented segments.
> - That gain does not depend on *which* neighbours the graph connects.
> - No placement rule usable before installation beats uniform random placement by more than 3% of weighted
>   cross-entropy.
> - What the sensor measures matters more: speed reconstructed from loop flow and occupancy detects critical
>   episodes but not their level, and averaging across lanes hides single-lane episodes.

What became of it:

- the bounded gain above a minimum coverage was not found (E9: no level of coverage makes propagation pay);
- the independence from *which* neighbours could not be tested where the graph itself adds nothing (E11);
- the comparison of placement rules was closed before it was run (note of 2026-11-08, 10:15) and replaced by
  E10-geo;
- the clause on what the sensor measures is Part A, unchanged.

Every part of the 2026-11-06 claim could fail, and each failure was declared in advance as a publishable result:

- a coverage curve with no threshold;
- a geometry that matters when the network is sparse;
- a placement rule that wins;
- a reconstruction that also measures level.

**What the deliverable contributes, and what it does not.** The deliverable UC-04 formulated these questions and
gave first answers: spacing 42–396 m without a monotone relation, randomised neighbours as good as kNN, one
coverage point (~44%). None of its R3 or geometry numbers enters the paper as a result (brief §9b), because:

- the randomisation preserved only the edge count (R0 §0.2);
- hidden nodes entered the loss (R0 §0.4);
- windows crossed calendar jumps (RC 1);
- Tables 34–35 could be regenerated only after fixing the pairing in the conserved script (RC 4).

The Phase 1 verdict is final (internal review, 2026-11-06, after the executor closed D6–D9): the training loop
was correct by documentary evidence, not by commit (R0 §13). It does not change the plan: no geometry or R3

### 1.2 Four questions, in the order every experiment must answer them

1. **What to measure (Part A).** Against an independent speed reference, what does a loop detector measure of
   congestion: level, detection, or both? How much of the disagreement on urban arterials is spatial matching,
   and what does lane aggregation hide?
2. **Which neighbours (E11, and criterion 3 of E9).** Does the geometry of the given graph matter, against a
   degree-preserving randomised adjacency, as a function of spacing and of coverage?
3. **How many (E9).** How does the forecast on uninstrumented segments change with the instrumented fraction
   *f*, and is there a coverage below which propagation stops helping? The answer is given in absolute wCE on
   hidden nodes, not only as a relative advantage: that is the number an operator needs to decide how many
   sensors to buy.
4. **Where (E10).** With the same number of sensors, does the rule that chooses the instrumented segments change
   the forecast on the uncovered ones, weighted by traffic? How much of an oracle's advantage do rules that need
   no data recover?

*Note of 2026-11-08 (internal review, 14:30).* Questions 2–4 are answered by Part B:

- question 2 by E11: undetermined, with no geometry effect where the graph adds nothing;
- question 3 by E9: no coverage threshold in 10–60%;
- question 4 by E12, E12-bis, E12-ter and E10-geo in place of E10. Placement changes proximity to a sensor,
  and proximity carries no forecast beyond the calendar.

Question 1 is Part A, still to be run. In the paper the questions are stated as RQ1–RQ4 in this order
(`paper4/paper/section_introduction.md`).

Reading rule, as in Paper 1 §III-B: every result is read on the sign of its interval and on the margin (§0,
rule 4). In Part B the interval is resampled jointly over seeds and corridor blocks; in Part A, over sensors or
sections.
