## 0. Method standard inherited from Paper 1

Not restated in full; these apply to every experiment of this document unchanged.

1. A pre-registration `protocol/paper4/E<n>.md` per experiment (question, design, arms, criterion with a
   numerical threshold, mandatory controls, what invalidates it, what changes in the paper, costs), saved with
   fingerprint and signed before the first run. Changes after results go to a "deviations" section.
2. Frozen records `CONGELAMENTO_E<n>.md` generated by script from the run files, with the fingerprints of every
   input; never written by hand. Corrections by regenerating or by a dated erratum appended at the end.
3. Per-cell predictions (node × origin × horizon × class) saved for every run; every number of the paper
   recomputable from them by one script (`paper4/analysis/main.py`), which prints the input fingerprints.
4. Joint resampling on every reported interval; "excludes zero" is the only decision formula; equivalence by
   TOST at ±3% relative, declared as a convention.
5. Selection (thresholds, hyper-parameters, checkpoints) on validation only, written to a fingerprinted file
   before any test metric is computed.
6. Preflight assertions before every campaign; a failing test is fixed, not commented.
7. Register of choices (value, provenance: discussed / inherited / imposed by data) and register of
   corrections, kept up to date; one tag per verified version, created by the author.
8. Costs measured in an isolated session (one process, idle machine, same machine for all arms); times
   measured under concurrency do not enter the cost table.

## 1. Thesis and questions

### 1.1 Thesis

The paper defends one claim, to be put to the test:

> **For congestion classification on road segments that have no sensor, what an operator can buy is coverage
> and the right measurement, not a clever placement.** Above a minimum fraction of instrumented network,
> propagation from instrumented neighbours adds a bounded gain on the uninstrumented segments; the gain does
> not depend on *which* neighbours the graph connects, and no placement rule usable before installation beats
> uniform random placement by more than 3% of weighted cross-entropy. What the sensor measures matters more:
> speed reconstructed from loop flow and occupancy detects critical episodes but not their level, and
> averaging across lanes hides single-lane episodes.

Every part of the claim can fail, and each failure is a publishable result stated as such: a coverage curve with
no threshold, a geometry that matters when the network is sparse, a placement rule that wins, a reconstruction
that also measures level.

**What the deliverable contributes, and what it does not.** The deliverable UC-04 formulated these questions and
gave first answers: spacing 42–396 m without a monotone relation, randomised neighbours as good as kNN, one
coverage point (~44%). After Phase 0 none of its R3 or geometry numbers enters the paper as a result (internal
review, 2026-10-10): the randomisation preserved only the edge count (R0 §0.2), hidden nodes entered the loss
(R0 §0.4), windows crossed calendar jumps (§2.1, row "Origin grid"), and Tables 34–35 cannot be regenerated
(R0 §0.5). The deliverable is cited as the source of the hypotheses, and the list of its defects goes in an
appendix. The data analyses of the technology half (Tables 64–69) train no model and are regenerated under
fingerprint; they enter if they reproduce.

### 1.2 Four questions, in the order every experiment must answer them

1. **How many (E9).** How does the forecast on uninstrumented segments change with the instrumented fraction
   *f*, and is there a coverage below which propagation stops helping? Absolute wCE on hidden nodes, not only
   the relative advantage: that is the number an operator needs to decide how many sensors to buy.
2. **Which neighbours (E11, and criterion 3 of E9).** Does the geometry of the given graph matter, against a
   degree-preserving randomised adjacency, as a function of spacing and of coverage?
3. **Where (E10).** With the same number of sensors, does the rule that chooses the instrumented segments change
   the forecast on the uncovered ones, weighted by traffic? How much of an oracle's advantage do rules that need
   no data recover?
4. **What to measure (4A).** Against an independent speed reference, what does a loop detector measure of
   congestion: level, detection, or both? How much of the disagreement on urban arterials is spatial matching,
   and what does lane aggregation hide?

Reading rule, as in Paper 1: a result counts only if its interval, resampled jointly over seeds and corridor
blocks, excludes zero.

## 3. Open questions for Gabriele

1. **One paper or two.** The Paper 1 protocol (§18) describes two halves with different status: 4A
   (technology, data only, a transport-engineering venue) and 4B (coverage, placement, geometry). This draft
   keeps them in one protocol with one thesis. Separate papers, or one? And which venue?
2. **Machine.** E9 alone is ≈ 115 h on the M3 Max. Is the NVIDIA Spark available for E9–E11 (D10 to the
   colleague)? All arms and costs must be on the same machine.
3. **E9 levels.** The review fixed "E9 at 4 levels (~115 h)". This draft reads them as *f* ∈ {10, 20, 40, 80}%:
   the brief's cut rule (remove 30 and 60) gives these four, and 4 × 5 × 3 × 3 = 180 trainings at ≈ 38 min ≈
   114 h matches the figure. Confirm?
4. **"Reduced" E10.** How is it reduced: fewer rules (e.g. random, grid-stratified, oracle), one level of *f*,
   or fewer cities?
5. **Corridor = grid cell?** The code builds masks from 6×6 grid cells; the brief speaks of contiguous
   corridors. Keep the grid (continuity with the deliverable) or define corridors on the road graph?
6. **Validation nodes.** With test-hidden nodes out of training (D1), checkpoint selection needs hidden nodes
   too. Proposed: a third, disjoint validation-hidden set of corridors. Alternative: select on observed nodes
   only, declared as a limit, as in Paper 1 ("new nodes are not supervised").
7. **Budget.** Fixed updates with an adequacy pilot, as in Paper 1, instead of 35 epochs with early stopping?
   It costs one pilot trajectory per arm.
8. **Static features.** Drop `lanes` and `max_capacity` (missing in Madrid and London), or keep them where they
   exist and declare the asymmetry?
9. **Calendar jumps and free-flow reference** (found while drafting §2.1). Adopting the admissible-origin rule
   means changing how `gomo_sparse.py` builds windows. On the loops the free-flow reference uses the test
   period; it matters only if GO-MO contexts come back into scope. Both go to the list of deliverable defects
   in the appendix: agreed?
10. **Brief §9b.** The review decisions of 10/10 cite "§9b of the P4 brief", which is not in the copy of the
    brief available here. If it exists, it should be attached.
