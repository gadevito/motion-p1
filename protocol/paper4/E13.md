## Question

E9 and E11 trained every arm for a fixed U = 1 000 updates chosen by a pilot rule, because on nodes never seen the validation wCE of G1 rose after ≈ 2 000–3 000 updates while the training loss kept falling (E11 §Training, E9 pilot). The small gain of the graph over no graph was therefore measured under one optimisation regime and one input set. E13 asks, on the same cities, node sets and horizons as E9 at f = 20 %:

**(A1-a)** Does the gain of spatial exchange, V(G1) − V(G0), change when each arm is given a per-split search over learning rate and regularisation, a long trajectory, and the checkpoint selected on validation-hidden nodes, instead of a fixed U?

**(A1-b)** Does it change when the node coordinates are admitted as input (regime I1), information that is available for a site without any measurement?

A0 asks, before any of this, whether the pipeline can be trusted to answer: it must exploit a spatial signal that is there, must not exploit one that is not, and must not see the hidden nodes anywhere.

## A0 — correctness checks (deterministic checks before the signature; A0.2–A0.3 after the tag, before A1; no test metric)

A0 repeats the E11 preflight (assertions 1–9, 4-bis, 5, 5-bis, determinism, guards) and the E9 controls on every (city, f = 0.20, seed), and adds:

**A0.1 Hidden-node invariance.** For one (city, seed), the dynamic values of every validation-hidden and test-hidden node are replaced by noise. Inputs, loss tensors, normalisation statistics, class weights and historical averages of a training batch must be identical to the bit; the trajectory of 50 updates must be identical to the bit. In regime I1 the coordinates of hidden nodes are **not** perturbed (they are legitimately known); the test is repeated perturbing them, and the inputs of the observed nodes must again be identical.

**A0.2 Synthetic data with a known dependence (after the tag, before A1).** On the Madrid graph and node sets of seed 0, two synthetic target processes are generated by `e13.py a0-synth` (generator and seed 20261118 fixed in the code before the tag): (i) **dependent**: each node's class sequence is a noisy copy, lagged by one step, of a latent process that propagates along the kernel adjacency of G1; (ii) **independent**: each node's class sequence is drawn from its own calendar profile with no cross-node term. G0 and G1 are trained with configuration C1 for 2 000 updates, validation every 250. **Criteria, fixed now** (the *best checkpoint* is the checkpoint ≥ 500 at which G1's validation wCE is minimal; V is read there): in (i) V(G1) on validation-hidden nodes ≥ +3 points at the best checkpoint: otherwise the full model does not exploit an available spatial signal and A1 does not start until the cause is found and corrected (dated note, new tag). In (ii), *an unexpected gain (V(G1) > +3 at any checkpoint after 500) triggers an investigation of the generator, of the information paths and of sampling variability; it is not, by itself, evidence of leakage or of exploitation of a nonexistent signal* — processes independent conditionally on the calendar can still have spatially structured local profiles, and an occasional advantage can come from finite variability and from selection among checkpoints. A1 does not start until the anomaly is explained in a dated note and the responsible author records the acceptance (`e13.py a0-accept --by NAME --note-commit H`, which binds the acceptance to the fingerprint of the A0.2 report); the generator, its parameters and seeds are fixed **before the tag** (with a statement of which information is absent by construction) and are not changed to make the check pass. V(G1) < −3 in (ii) is reported and investigated, not a block. The report states the outcomes and the explanation, if any.

**A0.3 Coordinates and node identity (regime I1; after the tag, before A1; descriptive, no criterion).** `max_speed` was removed in E11 because it acted as a node identifier and made G0 diverge (E11 D0 → D1). Coordinates may act the same way. Two declared sensitivities, on Madrid seed 0, validation only, both compared with the I0 curves of the same seed: (a) the I1 validation and training curves of G0 and G1 over 2 000 updates; (b) **coordinate permutation**: the (x, y) **pairs** of the observed nodes are permuted among the observed nodes once, with a random association fixed per node for the whole trajectory, kept when the node is a masked target and in every evaluation; graph, split, scalers, initialisation and batch order unchanged; the coordinates of validation-hidden nodes stay true (the comparison is therefore not symmetric between geography and arbitrary labels, and this is declared). *Similar validation performance with true and permuted coordinates does not establish that coordinates act as identifiers: the control measures sensitivity to the coordinate–node association and is interpreted jointly with I0 and with the training and validation curves.* No threshold is attached; the curves and the differences are reported in the A0 report and in the paper's appendix. The campaign measures I1 as declared whatever A0.3 shows. No test node is involved.

**A0.4 London files.** Fingerprints of the London data files differ from Madrid's (checked in E13-pre; re-asserted).

**A0.5 Validation and test disjointness across splits.** For each city, the five seeds' test-hidden sets and the development procedure are listed; the preflight asserts that no configuration or checkpoint choice for split s reads any node of test_s (nested tuning, §Training).

A0 outputs: `paper4/runs/e13_preflight.log` (before the signature; fingerprint in the signature line); `e13_a0_synth.json`, `e13_a0_identifier.json` (after the tag; fingerprints in a dated note under Deviations/Notes and in the frozen record).

## Design (A1)

| Factor | Levels | Note |
|---|---|---|
| City | Madrid (828 nodes), London (774), Melbourne (587) | as E9; grid G = 11, 11, 15 (E9 rule, unchanged) |
| Observed fraction f | **20 %** | targets observed / validation-hidden / test-hidden = 0.20 / 0.20 / 0.60, the E9 rule; node sets identical to E9's at f = 20 % for each seed (same stream k = 1) |
| Seed (outer split) | 0–4 | determines node sets, masks, batch order, graph streams, as E9 |
| Arm | G0 (no graph), G1 (aligned road graph, Gaussian kernel, as E9) | G1-rand is not repeated |
| Input regime | **I0**: the E9 inputs (dynamic channels, calendar, the zero channel of the removed static attribute); **I1**: I0 + two static channels, the node's UTM coordinates standardised with mean and standard deviation of the **observed nodes of the split** (not of all nodes) | the encoding is fixed here; no other static attribute |

**60 cells** (3 cities × 5 seeds × 2 arms × 2 regimes). Each cell receives four development trajectories and one final trajectory: **300 trainings** (240 + 60), P4R §8.

**Nested tuning per split (one development initialisation per configuration).** For cell (city, s, arm, regime), four configurations are trained from the development initialisation seed `rng([s, 7]).integers(2**31)`:

| Configuration | lr | dropout | weight decay |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 — **historical optimiser configuration** (Adam as in `p4b.py`, no weight decay) | 1e-3 | 0 | 0 |
| C2 | 1e-3 | 0.3 | 1e-4 |
| C3 | 3e-4 | 0 | 1e-4 |
| C4 | 3e-4 | 0.3 | 1e-4 |

C1 lets the search select the historical regime without added regularisation; the four configurations serve the selection, not a separate estimate of each hyperparameter's effect (the lr × dropout factorial is not balanced, and no such effect is reported). C1 is the historical *optimiser* configuration, not the historical setting: trajectory length, checkpoint selection and development initialisation differ. Dropout is the E11 D4 placement (block outputs). Each trajectory runs to U_max = 10 000 updates with a checkpoint every 500 (21 checkpoints, update 0 included), validation wCE on validation-hidden nodes of split s at each checkpoint (input = all observed nodes). **Selection, per cell:** the configuration and checkpoint with the minimum validation wCE; ties by the earlier checkpoint, then the lower configuration index. Nothing of split s's test-hidden nodes is read; nothing is shared between splits. **One development run per cell.** `e13.py dev` refuses a cell whose development run at the current cap exists; an attempt interrupted before its record is written is simply re-run (attempt counter in the record). A deliberate re-run requires a dated deviation note committed first and is launched with `--deviation <commit>`, which is stored in the record; the previous run stays in the index and the latest usable run is the one used.

**Final trajectory.** The selected configuration is retrained from the **final initialisation seed** `rng([s, 6])` (the E9 stream), same split, same masks and batch order, to U_max; the checkpoint is selected again on validation-hidden by the same rule (the final run's own curve). The test metric is computed once, at that checkpoint, after the signature. One final initialisation per split: the variance of the split and that of the initialisation are not separated, and the paper says so.

**Adequacy rule (fixed now) and extension.** The rule is applied to the **development** trajectories of a cell: the selected (configuration, checkpoint) must lie before the cap — the minimum of its validation curve not at the last checkpoint, and no improvement larger than 0.5 % of the validation wCE between the last two checkpoints. If it fails in any cell, **all four development trajectories of every cell of that (city, arm, regime)** are extended to 20 000 updates by `e13.py extend` (a fresh run to 20 000 with the same streams, whose first 21 checkpoints must reproduce the 10 000 curve within 1e-9 in validation wCE; the reproduction is recorded per configuration), the selection is repeated on the extended curves, and the final trajectories of those cells run to the same cap; a final run made before the extension is superseded and is not used. The extension is registered as a deviation with the list of cells and the budget note of P4R is updated. It can happen once per (city, arm, regime); a second failure is reported as a feasibility limit for those cells. *The final trajectory is assessed with the same adequacy diagnostics. A failure does not trigger further training, but the affected contrast cannot support a conclusion of limited gain under adequate training and is reported as inconclusive.* Cells that still fail after an extension are excluded from the adequacy-conditioned statements, not merely annotated; the counts of excluded cells are reported with every statement.

**Global lock before any test metric.** Test metrics are computed only when, for **all 60 cells**: the development selection and any extension are complete; the final trajectory exists, was trained from the **latest** development run (same run fingerprint, configuration and cap) and its selected state is intact; the adequacy report (`e13_adequacy.json`, which lists these identities and carries a fingerprint of them) is written and committed; `e13.py test` recomputes the report and refuses if it differs from the committed one; `require_signed` passes. Pending development failures (cap 10 000 not yet extended) block the lock; failures after the extension and final-fit failures do not block it but exclude the cell from the adequacy-conditioned statements. No cell's test metric is computed earlier than another's.

**Perimeter unchanged from E9/E11:** per-batch masks among observed cells only, u ~ U(0.6, 0.95); hidden nodes never in input, loss, normalisation, historical average or class weights; admissible origins, split 70/10/20 with purge; six random streams plus stream 7 for the development initialisation; architecture as PROTOCOLLO §2.3; all arms and regimes of a (city, s) on the same machine (the Mac, MPS).

**What differs from E9, declared:** the checkpoint selected on a long trajectory in place of a fixed U; weight decay; the search over lr and dropout; regime I1; f = 20 % only. The E9 results at f = 20 % (same node sets) are the historical comparison in §Secondary.

## Unit of analysis and inference (P4R §7)

Cell = (city, seed, corridor block, node, origin). wCE on test-hidden nodes per horizon, mean over the four horizons. V_c(a, regime) = mean over seeds of 100·(wCE(G0) − wCE(a))/wCE(G0), in points of the G0 wCE **of the same regime**; every contrast is a paired difference on identical targets, origins and masks.

**Resampling.** Two-way block bootstrap with units shared across splits: the spatial units are the corridor blocks (grid cells of the city's E9 grid), the temporal units are the ISO weeks of the test period. For each replicate **one** vector of multiplicities is drawn for the blocks and **one** for the weeks of the city, and the same multiplicities are applied to every split (seed), arm and regime, each on its own admissible test observations (a block counts in a split only where it holds that split's test-hidden nodes). The five splits are not resampled as units and are not treated as independent replicates: they overlap in nodes and in time, and sharing the multiplicities across them carries that dependence into the interval; V is the mean over the five splits and its interval is conditional on the five splits and on the observed fits (re-training variability is not in it; P4R §7). The seed-to-seed spread of each contrast is reported next to the interval. 4 000 replicates, generator `default_rng([20261118, city index])`, 95 % percentile intervals. This specification precedes the implementation of intervals, p-values and simultaneous bands in `e13.py`.

**p-values for Holm.** For each contrast, a centred bootstrap test: the replicate distribution of the contrast shifted to mean zero gives the null distribution; the two-sided p-value is the fraction of replicates at least as far from zero as the observed contrast. Holm at α = 0.05 is applied across the whole P4R family of 24 contrasts once A2/A3 are in; until then the six A1 p-values are reported unadjusted and labelled.

**Simultaneous bounds.** For a joint statement over the six A1 contrasts (for example "below the margin in every city"), the sup-t band from the same replicates (the 95 % quantile of the maximum standardised deviation over the six) is used instead of the six marginal intervals.

Cells and nodes without valid targets are excluded with their counts.

## Criteria

**Primary family (A1 part of the P4R §7 family of 24).** Six contrasts, V_c(G1, I0) and V_c(G1, I1) for each city, each with its interval. Holm is applied across the full family of 24 by the campaign-wide analysis once A2/A3 are in; `e13.py analysis` reports the six with marginal intervals and unadjusted p-values and its statement is **always labelled PROVISIONAL**: the confirmatory statement for the paper is produced only by the campaign-wide analysis after the family-wise correction, and a provisional positive reading is a candidate, not a result. Readings on the two scales of E11 (sign; margin of 3 points, the declared convention for the classification task). The statement is derived mechanically:

| Statement | Condition |
|---|---|
| "spatial exchange helps at 20 % under adequate training" | V_c(G1, I0) favourable and ≥ 3 in at least 2 cities |
| "gain below the prespecified 3-point margin at 20 % under adequate training" | the simultaneous upper bound of V_c(G1, I0) over the three cities is < 3 (a measurable gain smaller than the margin may exist and is reported as estimated), **and** every cell entering the three contrasts passes the final-fit adequacy diagnostics |
| "undetermined" | every other case, reported with counts |

**Secondary, declared, paired.** (i) **Interaction with coordinates:** Δ_c = V_c(G1, I1) − V_c(G1, I0), with interval from the same replicates; the claim "coordinates change the gain" requires Δ_c's interval to exclude zero in at least two cities; a significant V in one regime and not in the other is not evidence of interaction. (ii) **Static benefit at fixed arm:** 100·(wCE(a, I0) − wCE(a, I1))/wCE(a, I0) for a ∈ {G0, G1}, with interval. (iii) **Training regime sensitivity:** V_c(G1, I0) of E13 next to V_c(G1) of E9 at f = 20 % (same node sets and seeds): a comparison of regimes, not a re-analysis of E9; the frozen E9 record is not touched. (iv) **Selected configurations and checkpoints**, tabulated per cell, with the validation curves in the appendix: this is the evidence on adequacy that the reviewer asked for.

**Reported without a criterion:** absolute wCE and distance from ln 3 per cell (E9 criterion (b)); red recall at 5 % false alarms; every quantity per horizon; the A0.3 identifier control.

## Mandatory controls

The E11 preflight and E9 controls at f = 20 %, plus A0.1–A0.5; the number of updates of every trajectory equals the cap of its cell (10 000, or 20 000 after a registered extension) within 1 %, with a checkpoint every 500 (21 or 41); per-cell prediction files; run folders named by configuration fingerprint; the development and final initialisation seeds differ and are logged; no test metric computed before the global lock conditions and `require_signed` pass.

## What invalidates the experiment

Everything listed in E9 and E11, plus: a configuration added, removed or changed after any test metric; a checkpoint or configuration chosen on any test node or on another split; the cap raised for a subset of the cells of a (city, arm, regime), or after any test metric; a test metric computed before the global lock; the coordinate encoding changed after the signature; development trajectories reused as final; A0.2 failed and A1 started anyway; arms or regimes of one (city, seed) on different machines.

## Cost

From E13-pre (Mac, MPS): G0 0.062 s/update, G1 0.131 s/update; fixed cost per checkpoint ≈ 1–2 s. 300 trajectories × 10 000 updates: G0 150 × (620 + 21×1) ≈ 27 h; G1 150 × (1 310 + 21×2) ≈ 56 h; **≈ 83 h of process time** on the Mac, within the calendar at the full grid (P4R outcome (a)). A0.2 (4 trainings) and A0.3 (6 trainings): 10 short trainings of 2 000 updates, < 1 h. An extension of a (city, arm, regime) re-runs from zero to 20 000 all four configurations of its five cells (20 trajectories) and then its five finals: for a G1 group ≈ 20 × 20 000 × 0.131 s + 5 × 20 000 × 0.131 s ≈ 18 h; for a G0 group ≈ 9 h; registered with the extension.

## What changes in the paper

The A1 paragraph answers the "inadequate training / single input set" objection with numbers: the selected budgets and configurations per cell, the validation curves and the adequacy diagnostics, and V with and without coordinates. "Helps" or "gain below the prespecified margin" is read as in E9, now under a tuned regime and conditioned on adequacy; the comparison with E9 at f = 20 % is a comparison between two training regimes as a whole (trajectory length, selection, initialisation, search), not an isolation of the budget U. The classification keeps the G0/G1 family (P4R §1); the multi-architecture comparison is E14.
